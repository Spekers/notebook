 Born in 1935 in Mie Prefec­ture, Isao Taka­hata gradu­ated from the Univer­sity of Tokyo (Depart­ment of French Liter­at­ure) in 1959. That same year, he joined Toei Anim­a­tion. In 1968, he direc­ted his first feature film for theat­rical release: The Great Adven­ture of Horus, Prince of the Sun (1968). In 1971, Taka­hata joined the studio A Produc­tion. In 1974, he direc­ted the 52 epis­odes of Heidi, Girl of the Alps, an anim­ated tele­vi­sion series adap­ted from a clas­sic of chil­dren’s liter­at­ure. He later direc­ted all epis­odes of the TV series From The Apen­nines to The Andes (1976) and Anne of Green Gables (1979), also based on chil­dren’s novels. He direc­ted the feature films Jarinko Chie (1981) and Gauche the Cellist (1982) the follow­ing year. Hayao Miyaza­ki’s Naus­icaä of the Valley of the Wind, released in 1984, was the first feature film he produced.
Taka­hata co-foun­ded Studio Ghibli with Hayao Miyazaki and Toshio Suzuki in 1985. For this studio, he direc­ted several films: Grave of the Fire­flies (1988), Only Yester­day (1991), Pom Poko (1994), and My Neigh­bors the Yama­das (1999). The Tale of The Prin­cess Kaguya (2013), his final master­piece, was selec­ted for the Direct­ors’ Fort­night at Cannes (2014) and was nomin­ated for the Academy Award for Best Anim­ated Feature (2015). Taka­hata was awar­ded the Honor­ific Leopard at Locarno Inter­na­tional Film Fest­ival (2009).
Isao Taka­hata was also the author of numer­ous works, includ­ing Eiga wo Tsukur­in­agara Kangaeta Koto (Reflec­tions While Making Films, not trans­lated), Jyuni-Seiki no Anim­a­tion (12th Century Anim­a­tion, not trans­lated), Anim­a­tion Ori ni Furete (Anim­a­tion, on Vari­ous Occa­sions, not trans­lated), among others.
