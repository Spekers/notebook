()  © guillaumepython    ()  © guillaumepython    ()  © guillaumepython             An exclus­ive section at mudac: Isao Taka­hata – A Life and a Body of Work in Dialogue with the West This part of the exhib­i­tion explores Isao Taka­hata’s decis­ive rela­tion­ship with the West, partic­u­larly the French-speak­ing world. It retraces his discov­ery of Préver­t’s poetic real­ism – a found­a­tion of both his aesthetic and polit­ical engage­ment – and presents his in-depth research into The Shep­herd­ess and The Chim­ney-Sweep (1953), through original docu­ments that shed light on the origins of his voca­tion. It then exam­ines the unpre­ced­en­ted chal­lenge of adapt­ing clas­sic West­ern stor­ies into anim­a­tion – Heidi, Girl of the Alps (1974), From The Apen­nines to The Andes (1976), Anne of Green Gables (1979) – combin­ing ethno­graphic rigour with docu­ment­ary ambi­tion. Finally, it high­lights Taka­hata’s exchanges with West­ern artists (Grim­ault, Ocelot, Back, Norstein and many others), his influ­ence on world anim­a­tion and his major role in estab­lish­ing anim­ated film as a recog­nised art form. This jour­ney illus­trates the import­ance of sustained cultural exchange, portray­ing Taka­hata as a key figure in inter­cul­tural dialogue and a source of inspir­a­tion across borders.       Isao Taka­hata   ()  Isao Taka­hata (1935–2018) is regarded as one of the great masters of world anim­a­tion. Co-founder of Studio Ghibli along­side Hayao Miyazaki in 1985, he had already, from the 1960s onwards, developed a body of work that is at once exact­ing, poetic and pion­eer­ing. Through his deeply human­istic vision, his narrat­ive sens­it­iv­ity and his daring formal approach, Taka­hata rein­ven­ted the language of anim­ated work, liber­at­ing it from its tradi­tional codes and turn­ing it into an art form in its own right, recog­nised for what he himself called “the inven­tion of anim­ated real­ity”.  Biography           Born in 1935 in Mie Prefec­ture, Isao Taka­hata gradu­ated from the Univer­sity of Tokyo (Depart­ment of French Liter­at­ure) in 1959. That same year, he joined Toei Anim­a­tion. In 1968, he direc­ted his first feature film for theat­rical release: The Great Adven­ture of Horus, Prince of the Sun (1968). In 1971, Taka­hata joined the studio A Produc­tion. In 1974, he direc­ted the 52 epis­odes of Heidi, Girl of the Alps , an anim­ated tele­vi­sion series adap­ted from a clas­sic of chil­dren’s liter­at­ure. He later direc­ted all epis­odes of the TV series From The Apen­nines to The Andes (1976) and Anne of Green Gables (1979), also based on chil­dren’s novels. He direc­ted the feature films Jarinko Chie (1981) and Gauche the Cellist (1982) the follow­ing year. Hayao Miyaza­ki’s Naus­icaä of the Valley of the Wind , released in 1984, was the first feature film he produced. Taka­hata co-foun­ded Studio Ghibli with Hayao Miyazaki and Toshio Suzuki in 1985. For this studio, he direc­ted several films: Grave of the Fire­flies (1988), Only Yester­day (1991), Pom Poko (1994), and My Neigh­bors the Yama­das (1999). The Tale of The Prin­cess Kaguya (2013), his final master­piece, was selec­ted for the Direct­ors ’Fort­night at Cannes (2014) and was nomin­ated for the Academy Award for Best Anim­ated Feature (2015). Taka­hata was awar­ded the Honor­ific Leopard at Locarno Inter­na­tional Film Fest­ival (2009).  Isao Taka­hata was also the author of numer­ous works, includ­ing  Eiga wo Tsukur­in­agara Kangaeta Koto  (Reflec­tions While Making Films, not trans­lated),  Jyuni-Seiki no Anim­a­tion  (12th Century Anim­a­tion, not trans­lated),  Anim­a­tion Ori ni Furete  (Anim­a­tion, on Vari­ous Occa­sions, not trans­lated), among others.
